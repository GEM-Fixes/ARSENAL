# Night attack on a perimeter

**Teaches:** Short vision favours whoever is closest and quietest. MGs on fixed lines hold the perimeter.

**Map needs:** night or fog, perimeter, scrub. **Win:** The attacker takes the command-post flag.

## Sides
**Attacker:** Japanese infantry, 1942 (reported doctrine), company shape, fidelity 0.8. Flanking and night attacks; at high fidelity it charges when losing. All doctrine here is reported by the US and UK.
  - Fake strength in front with noise and automatic fire while the main force goes round a flank.
  - Under a holding attack, send LMG and grenade patrols round the flanks into the rear.
  - Prefer attacks when vision is short.
  - When a group is losing, charge instead of falling back. (larp lore)
**Defender:** US rifle battalion, 1944, platoon shape, fidelity 0.6. Methodical; strong with support weapons, slow without them.
  - Lead with fire: mortars and MGs on the objective before any rifle squad moves.
  - Put the main line on the reverse slope when the forward slope is under observed fire.
  - Counter infiltration with posts that hold alone and a reserve that strikes hard.

## Doctrine
- **Night attack:** Reconnoiter at least the night before. Go straight at the objective: mixing frontal attack and envelopment at night usually produces confusion and friendly fire. Stay in column until 100 to 200 yards out, silent and marked. Units stopped by darkness dig in all-round.

## What a good bot does
- **Night or low vision means straight ahead and simple** (tac.night)
  - Keep groups in column and deploy only close to the objective.
  - No envelopment: one direct assault on a point already scouted.
  - Narrow each weapon's sector and raise density on the line.
  - A group that stops loses its order: set all-round defense where it stands.
  - Patrol the gaps between held points.
- **Machine guns in enfilade, crossing fire, no gap at the flanks** (tac.mg-defense)
  - Site MGs on the flanks of the position, firing across the front, not straight ahead.
  - Neighbouring MGs' fields cross; check each flank is covered.
  - Heavy MGs take the long fixed lines; light MGs fill the gaps near posts.
